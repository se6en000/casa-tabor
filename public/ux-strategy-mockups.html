Current Pain Points
  • Mental Model Schism: "Needs You" (Kiosk Rail), "Briefing" (Narrative), and "Action Hub" (Admin Table) present the exact same data in three unaligned ways.
  • Micro-action Clutter: 3 icon buttons (Done, Snooze, Dislike) are crammed on 44px rows with nested click targets, causing accidental taps.
  • Briefing Identity Crisis: Briefing duplicates the entire day's raw calendar timeline, making it redundant with the Home screen.
World-Class Principles
  • The 3 Speeds of Attention: Tailor each surface for its precise physical context: 3-second glance (countertop), 60-second orientation (morning coffee), 3-minute resolution (evening triage).
  • Single Card Anatomy: A single, universally recognizable Attention Card token across all views.
  • Generous Tactility: Zero tiny buttons on ambient cards; primary action is 1-tap, secondary is swipe or long-press with 4-second optimistic undo.
The Target Experience
  • Warm Family Hospitality: Feels like a discreet private concierge, not Jira or an enterprise inbox.
  • Contextual Cohesion: What you see on the Kiosk smoothly expands into the Morning Briefing and deepens into the Action Hub without resetting your mental map.
  • Zero Redundancy: Briefing delivers high-level logistics intelligence; Home delivers real-time schedule; Action Hub delivers bulk resolution.

The 3 Speeds of Household Attention

A world-class family OS respects where and how the user interacts. The three features should not compete; they represent three progressive depths of engagement.
